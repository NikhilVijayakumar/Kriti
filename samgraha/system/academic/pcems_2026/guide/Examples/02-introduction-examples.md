# Introduction Examples

> *Source: PCEMS 2026 Sample Papers*

## Purpose

The introduction establishes why the paper matters and what it contributes. This document provides annotated excerpts from accepted sample papers, showing effective introduction structures.

## Example 1: IoT-Based Smart Food Grain Warehouse

### Opening Paragraph (Problem Significance)

> "Currently, every developing country is focusing on agriculture and farming to expand and strengthen its economy. A farmer must adhere to a complex process in order to achieve and maintain the best output results. This procedure may involve planting, harvesting, drying, storing, and selling. However, as we can see, storage and store management perform an important part in deciding the grade of product that a farmer is ultimately able to produce."

**Analysis**:
- Opens with broad context (agriculture in developing countries)
- Narrows to specific problem (storage management)
- Establishes relevance (economic impact)
- Uses formal engineering tone

### Problem Statement Paragraph

> "The system will keep track of, identify, examine, and manage all accidents that take place in the go-downs. Through the use of various sensors and a microcontroller that will automatically reflect the data on the desktop system, the produced device will assist in monitoring the warehouse."

**Analysis**:
- States what the system does
- Describes the technical approach at a high level
- Sets up the methodology section

## Example 2: Credit Card Fraud Detection

### Opening with Data

> "Fraud is deceiving someone into intentionally giving up on their properties, money, or any legal rights, resulting in financial or personal gain for the illegitimate person. Many fraud types occur in our daily lives, including credit card fraud... In 2020 the fraud in credit card cases had increased to 45,120."

**Analysis**:
- Opens with definition
- Provides quantitative data to establish significance
- Narrows from general fraud to credit card fraud specifically
- Uses statistics to justify the research

### Gap Identification

> "Due to the rise of online transactions, credit card usage is increasing. Most of the transactions are online, which requires credit/debit card transactions. Without knowing, credit cards play an essential role in our daily lives."

**Analysis**:
- Identifies the trend driving the problem
- Establishes real-world relevance
- Sets up the need for automated detection

## Example 3: Nanotube JLFET Investigation

### Technical Opening

> "Field effect transistors are a major contributor to the present technology. The conventional MOSFET devices used are outdated as they are not compatible due to their scaling issues [1]. The short channel effects which come into play at sub-micron regime makes conventional MOSFET less desirable for use."

**Analysis**:
- Opens with technical context
- Cites foundational work immediately
- States the specific problem (scaling issues)
- Uses precise technical language

### Gap Identification

> "Though the ON current of the NT JLFETs is pretty decent (10-7 amp), the OFF current suffers, it keeps on increasing as we scale down the channel below 20 nm. The main factor in increased OFF current is Lateral BTBT."

**Analysis**:
- Identifies specific limitation with quantitative data
- States the physical mechanism causing the limitation
- Sets up the investigation as addressing this specific gap

## Introduction Structure Templates

### Template A: Problem-First

```
Paragraph 1: Why does this problem matter? (context + data)
Paragraph 2: What has been tried? (existing approaches)
Paragraph 3: What is missing? (limitations of current work)
Paragraph 4: What does this paper contribute? (proposed solution)
```

### Template B: Gap-First

```
Paragraph 1: What is the current state of the art?
Paragraph 2: What limitation prevents further progress?
Paragraph 3: How does this paper address that limitation?
Paragraph 4: What are the specific contributions?
```

## Common Introduction Mistakes (from sample papers)

### Weak Opening
> "In today's world, technology is advancing rapidly."

**Why it fails**: Vague, adds no information, could apply to any paper.

### Strong Opening
> "In 2020 the fraud in credit card cases had increased to 45,120. The rise in both online transactions has led to more fraud in credit cards."

**Why it works**: Specific data, quantified problem, establishes urgency.

### Missing Contribution Statement
Some sample papers lack an explicit "this paper proposes" statement. This is a weakness. Always include a paragraph near the end of the introduction that explicitly states the contribution.

### Effective Contribution Statement
> "This technical paper describes the creation of a smart grain storage system that uses ultrasonic mouse repellent to control rodent while also monitoring air conditions."

**Why it works**: Specific about what was built and what it does.
