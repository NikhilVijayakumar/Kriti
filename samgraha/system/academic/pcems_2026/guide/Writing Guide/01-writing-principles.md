# Writing Principles

> *Source: PCEMS Publication Philosophy + PCEMS 2026 Template + Sample Paper Analysis*

## Purpose

This document establishes the core writing methodology for PCEMS 2026 manuscripts. Every section of a PCEMS paper exists to communicate a specific engineering contribution. Writing is the mechanism for organizing validated research into a publication-ready narrative, not the mechanism for discovering research.

## Core Principles

### 1. Research Precedes Writing

Writing should never become the mechanism for discovering research. The research must already be complete before publication begins. A paper organizes existing research into a publication-ready narrative.

### 2. Every Claim Requires Evidence

Every technical claim must be supported by appropriate evidence. Claims must never rely on assumptions, opinions, or unsupported statements. Evidence includes experimental results, comparative evaluation, performance measurements, mathematical derivations, architectural reasoning, algorithms, implementation analysis, published literature, or reproducible experiments.

### 3. Every Section Has a Defined Purpose

Each section answers a distinct question:

| Section | Primary Question |
|---------|-----------------|
| Introduction | Why does this problem matter? |
| Literature Review | What already exists? |
| Methodology | How does the proposed solution work? |
| Findings | What was observed? |
| Discussion | Why are the results important? |
| Conclusion | What has been achieved? |

Sections must never duplicate information or contain unrelated discussion.

### 4. Clarity Over Complexity

Technical writing must maximize understanding. Complex language must never be used to create an appearance of scientific sophistication. Readers must understand what problem is being solved, why it matters, how it is solved, why the proposed solution works, and what evidence supports the conclusions.

### 5. Contribution-Oriented Writing

Every paper must communicate a clearly identifiable contribution. Readers must never finish a paper wondering what has actually been contributed. The contribution must remain visible throughout the manuscript and be reinforced consistently across Introduction, Methodology, Findings, and Conclusion.

## Tone and Register

PCEMS papers require formal engineering prose. The writing must be:

- **Precise**: Avoid vague statements, ambiguous terminology, and exaggerated claims
- **Objective**: Present findings without subjective interpretation
- **Concise**: Eliminate unnecessary words without sacrificing clarity
- **Consistent**: Maintain uniform terminology, abbreviations, and notation throughout

### Language to Avoid

| Avoid | Use Instead |
|-------|-------------|
| "It is important to note that" | [Delete — state the fact directly] |
| "In this paper, we will try to" | "This paper presents" |
| "A lot of" | "Numerous" or "Many" |
| "Basically" | [Delete] |
| "Very" | [Delete or use specific qualifier] |
| "Thing" | Use the specific technical term |
| "Good results" | "Results meeting the target threshold of X%" |

### AI-Generated Language Flags

Reviewers actively scan for AI-generated text. Avoid these patterns:

- Overuse of "delve," "landscape," "tapestry," "crucial," "paramount"
- Excessive hedging ("it should be noted that," "it is worth mentioning")
- Repetitive sentence structures
- Unnatural transition phrases ("In the realm of," "In the landscape of")

## Paragraph Structure

Each paragraph must serve a single purpose. A well-structured paragraph follows this pattern:

1. **Topic sentence**: States the paragraph's claim
2. **Evidence**: Provides supporting data, citations, or reasoning
3. **Analysis**: Interprets the evidence in context
4. **Transition**: Connects to the next paragraph

### Paragraph Length

- Target: 4-8 sentences per paragraph
- Maximum: 12 sentences (split into multiple paragraphs)
- Minimum: 2 sentences (merge with adjacent paragraph if shorter)

## Sentence Construction

### Prefer Active Voice

| Passive | Active |
|---------|--------|
| "The experiment was conducted by the researchers" | "The researchers conducted the experiment" |
| "It was found that the accuracy improved" | "The accuracy improved" |
| "The data was collected from" | "We collected data from" |

### Limit Sentence Length

- Target: 15-25 words per sentence
- Maximum: 40 words (split into two sentences)
- If a sentence contains more than one main idea, split it

### Use Parallel Structure

| Non-Parallel | Parallel |
|--------------|----------|
| "The method is fast, accurate, and it has low cost" | "The method is fast, accurate, and low-cost" |
| "To detect, classify, and the system identifies" | "The system detects, classifies, and identifies" |

## Section Transitions

Every section must connect logically to the next. Use explicit transition sentences at the end of each section or the beginning of the next.

### Effective Transition Patterns

- **Problem → Solution**: "To address these limitations, this paper proposes..."
- **Gap → Contribution**: "Existing approaches do not address X. This paper fills that gap by..."
- **Result → Implication**: "These results indicate that..."
- **Finding → Next Section**: "The following section describes the methodology used to obtain these results."

## Word Count Guidelines

Based on analysis of 11 accepted PCEMS 2026 sample papers:

| Metric | Range | Target |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Total paper length | 2,400-4,800 words | 3,200-4,000 words |
| Abstract | 150-250 words | 200 words |
| Introduction | 400-800 words | 500-600 words |
| Literature Review | 400-800 words | 500-600 words |
| Methodology | 600-1,200 words | 800-1,000 words |
| Findings | 600-1,200 words | 800-1,000 words |
| Conclusion | 150-400 words | 200-300 words |

These are guidelines, not rigid requirements. The content dictates the length.

## Revision Checklist

Before finalizing any section, verify:

- [ ] Every claim has supporting evidence
- [ ] Every abbreviation is defined at first use
- [ ] Every figure and table is referenced in the text
- [ ] Every in-text citation appears in the reference list
- [ ] Terminology is consistent throughout
- [ ] Sentences are clear and concise
- [ ] Paragraphs follow the topic-evidence-analysis structure
- [ ] Section transitions are explicit
- [ ] No informal or AI-generated language patterns
