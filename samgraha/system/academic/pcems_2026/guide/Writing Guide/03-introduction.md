# Introduction Writing Guide

> *Source: PCEMS 2026 Template + Documentation-Standards/02-introduction-standards.md + Sample Paper Analysis*

## Purpose

The Introduction establishes the context, identifies the gap, and states the paper's contribution. It answers: "Why does this problem matter, and what does this paper do about it?"

## Structure

A PCEMS Introduction follows a funnel pattern: broad context → specific gap → paper contribution.

### Required Elements

1. **Problem Context** (1-2 paragraphs): Establish the broader problem space
2. **Gap Identification** (1 paragraph): Identify what existing approaches fail to address
3. **Paper Contribution** (1 paragraph): State what this paper proposes
4. **Paper Organization** (optional, 1 paragraph): Outline the remaining sections

### Template

```markdown
[Introduce the broader problem space]
[Provide relevant background and motivation]
[Identify the specific gap being addressed]
[State the objectives and contributions of this manuscript]
[Outline the paper organization — optional]
```

## Content Requirements

### Problem Context

Begin with the engineering problem. Establish why it matters by citing:
- The scale of the problem (statistics, prevalence)
- The impact (economic, social, technical)
- The current state of practice

**Example pattern**:
> "X is a significant problem affecting Y. In Z year, the impact reached W [citation]. Current approaches to addressing X suffer from limitation L [citation]."

### Gap Identification

After establishing context, identify what existing work fails to address. The gap must be:
- Specific (not "existing work is insufficient")
- Verifiable (a reader can confirm the gap by reading the cited works)
- Relevant (the gap matters for the problem domain)

**Example pattern**:
> "While numerous studies have addressed X [citations], existing approaches fail to account for Y. This limitation results in Z."

### Paper Contribution

State the contribution explicitly. Do not force readers to infer what was contributed. Use direct language:

**Example pattern**:
> "This paper proposes [method name] for [application]. The proposed approach [key innovation]. The main contributions are:
> 1. [Contribution 1]
> 2. [Contribution 2]
> 3. [Contribution 3]"

### Paper Organization (Optional)

If included, keep it to one sentence per remaining section:

> "The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section II reviews related work. Section III describes the proposed methodology. Section IV presents the experimental results. Section V concludes the paper."

## Typography

- **Section heading**: Heading 1 (Arial, 12pt, Bold)
- **Body text**: Arial, 11pt
- **Single column format**
- **First paragraph**: No indentation (or consistent indentation throughout)

## Length Guidelines

Based on sample paper analysis:

| Metric | Range | Target |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Word count | 400-800 words | 500-600 words |
| Paragraphs | 3-6 | 4-5 |
| Citations | 5-15 | 8-12 |

## Writing Strategy

### Do

- Start with the problem, not the solution
- Use specific numbers and citations to establish significance
- Make the gap explicit and verifiable
- State contributions as a numbered list
- Connect the gap to the contribution directly

### Do Not

- Begin with dictionary definitions ("X is defined as...")
- Begin with overly broad statements ("Since the dawn of time...")
- Introduce methodology details in the Introduction
- Present results in the Introduction
- Use informal language ("In this paper, we will try to...")
- Exceed one page in length

## Common Patterns from Sample Papers

Analysis of 11 accepted PCEMS 2026 papers reveals these Introduction patterns:

1. **Problem-first opening**: 9 of 11 papers begin with the problem statement
2. **Citation density**: Average of 10 citations in the Introduction
3. **Contribution statement**: 8 of 11 papers explicitly list contributions
4. **Organization paragraph**: 6 of 11 papers include a section outline
5. **Length**: Average of 550 words

## Revision Checklist

- [ ] Problem context is established with specific evidence
- [ ] Gap is explicitly identified and verifiable
- [ ] Contributions are stated as a numbered list
- [ ] Every claim has at least one citation
- [ ] No methodology details appear in the Introduction
- [ ] No results appear in the Introduction
- [ ] Length is within 400-800 words
- [ ] First sentence engages the reader with the problem
