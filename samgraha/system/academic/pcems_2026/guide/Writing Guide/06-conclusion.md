# Conclusion Writing Guide

> *Source: PCEMS 2026 Template + Documentation-Standards/05-conclusion-standards.md + Sample Paper Analysis*

## Purpose

The Conclusion summarizes the paper's contribution and findings. It answers: "What has been achieved, and what comes next?"

## Structure

A PCEMS Conclusion follows a summary-forward pattern: contribution recap → key findings → implications → future work.

### Required Elements

1. **Contribution Recap** (1-2 sentences): Restate what the paper proposed
2. **Key Findings** (2-3 sentences): Summarize the main results
3. **Implications** (1-2 sentences): State what the results mean for the field
4. **Future Work** (1-2 sentences): Identify next steps

### Template

```markdown
[Restate the paper's contribution]
[Summarize the key findings and their significance]
[Discuss the implications of the results]
[Identify directions for future work]
```

## Content Requirements

### Contribution Recap

Begin by restating what the paper proposed. This should mirror the Introduction's contribution statement but in past tense (the work is now complete).

**Example pattern**:
> "This paper proposed [method name] for [application]. The approach [key innovation]."

### Key Findings

Summarize the most important results. Focus on the headline numbers:

**Example pattern**:
> "Experimental results demonstrated that the proposed method achieves [metric] of [value], outperforming existing approaches by [margin]."

### Implications

State what the results mean for the field or for practitioners:

**Example pattern**:
> "These results indicate that [implication]. The proposed approach can be applied to [broader application]."

### Future Work

Identify specific, actionable next steps:

**Example pattern**:
> "Future work will focus on [specific direction]. Additionally, [second direction] will be investigated."

## Typography

- **Section heading**: Heading 1 (Arial, 12pt, Bold)
- **Body text**: Arial, 11pt
- **Single paragraph** (preferred) or 2-3 short paragraphs

## Length Guidelines

Based on sample paper analysis:

| Metric | Range | Target |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Word count | 150-400 words | 200-300 words |
| Paragraphs | 1-3 | 1-2 |

## Writing Strategy

### Do

- Restate the contribution in past tense
- Include specific numbers (accuracy, improvement over baseline)
- Connect findings back to the problem stated in the Introduction
- Keep it concise (one page maximum)
- End with future work

### Do Not

- Introduce new results or data
- Repeat the Methodology section
- Make claims not supported by the findings
- Use vague language ("the results were good")
- Exceed one page

## Common Patterns from Sample Papers

1. **Structure**: Most papers use a single paragraph for Conclusion
2. **Future work**: 8 of 11 papers include future work directions
3. **Length**: Average of 250 words
4. **Numbers**: 7 of 11 papers include specific performance metrics

## Example

> "This paper proposed a credit card fraud detection system using machine learning classification techniques. The proposed approach was evaluated on a dataset of 284,807 transactions, with Random Forest achieving the highest accuracy of 99.95%. The results demonstrate that ensemble methods outperform individual classifiers for fraud detection tasks. Future work will focus on real-time detection systems and adaptive learning approaches."

## Revision Checklist

- [ ] Contribution is restated in past tense
- [ ] Key findings include specific numbers
- [ ] Findings connect back to the Introduction's problem statement
- [ ] Future work is specific and actionable
- [ ] No new results or data are introduced
- [ ] Length is within 150-400 words
- [ ] The section can stand alone as a summary of the paper
